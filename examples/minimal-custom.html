What changes

Before

One job exports every account in a single pass. If it overruns, nobody hears about it until a customer asks.

After

A worker takes one account at a time from a queue and writes a result per account. A missing result is visible the same day.

A plan nobody can check is a wish list.

How a page gets built

Template

base.html holds the components, the light and dark modes and the print rules. Colours and font come from the theme chosen in the metadata; your text goes where the placeholder is.

Script

base.js handles the generic parts: tabs from the keyboard, disclosures, steps with previous and next, the answer to copy. Page-specific interactions go in a script inside the content.

Check

check.py opens the page in Chromium at two widths, light and dark, tries every tab, prints, and loads the page without JavaScript.

The steps

Four steps, from the plan to the cleanup. After step 2 both the old job and the worker run, so stopping there loses nothing.

Step 1. Write the plan

The plan file comes before any change to the export.

Step 2. Add the worker

safe state

The worker is deployed next to the old job and handles no account yet.

Step 3. Switch accounts over

safe state

Accounts move to the worker a few at a time; the old job skips the ones already moved.

Step 4. Remove the old job

Only after every account has a worker result for the agreed period.

Risks

A queue that grows faster than the worker drains it shows up as accounts without a result. The report lists them by name.

Each result row is keyed by account and night, so a retry overwrites instead of adding a second row.

During step 3 an account belongs to exactly one of them. A list of moved accounts says which.

Until step 4, moving an account back is one line in the list of moved accounts. After step 4, the old job comes back from version control.
